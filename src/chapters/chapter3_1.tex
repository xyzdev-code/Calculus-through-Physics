\section{Essence of calculus}


Perhaps framing an established pedagogical subject like calculus through the unconventional lens of physics is not optimal, but I will try my best. One of the most infamous problems in physics is the Brachistochrome curve, which has a deceptively simple problem statement:
\begin{problem}
  \textit{(Brachistochrome curve)} Find the curve that connects a point A and a lower point B, where B is not directly below A, on which a ball slides frictionlessly under the influence of a uniform gravitational field from A to B in the shortest time.\sidenote{Uniform gravitational field means that no matter the position of the ball, the gravitational force acting on the ball is the same.}
\end{problem}


What do you think is the fastest path? Perhaps a straight line, a quarter circle?
